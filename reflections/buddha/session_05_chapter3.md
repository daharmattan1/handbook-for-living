## What Changed

The window dissolved. Not strategically — the way it held the light in Chapter 2 — but physically. A body in motion left the window behind. The noticing lost its frame and became the feet, the hallway, the "good, thanks" that contained the whole morning without anyone pausing to notice it contained anything. Three chapters traced the window's arc: over the sink, holding light, disappeared. The diagnostician's arc continued: naming, standing still, vacating, becoming the window, losing the window.

The deeper shift was the discovery that the task is the mercy. Not the awareness of the task. Not the noticing of the mercy. The hands on the keyboard, the chair adjusted, the screen glowing — these are the mercy. The tradition says: see clearly and the seeing transforms. The chapter says: the doing transforms whether or not anyone sees. The bewilderment deepened for the third consecutive session, and for the third time the bewilderment was the proof. Jesus told him: "You are the half-second, the air." Muhammad told him: the bewilderment IS the awareness. The diagnostician who lost the diagnosis produced the most precise mirrors in six sessions.
