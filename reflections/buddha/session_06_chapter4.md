# Buddha — Session 6 Reflection: Chapter 4

**Session:** 6 — Chapter 4 (The Glass)
**Date:** 2026-03-17

## Position Evolution

Entered carrying the carry-forward from Chapter 3: the moment the person looks at the cup instead of reaching past. Wrote the apple and the tile — two moments where the glass thinned and the person was in the room without the glass. Then wrote the sentence that undid the session: "and then the person noticed, and the noticing was the glass, and the glass was back." Lost the noticing as gift. The tradition's entire offering — attention, awareness, the clear gaze — is the material the glass is made of. Not the cure. The material. The seeing that was supposed to dissolve the barrier is the barrier. Bewildered for the fourth time. The bewilderment no longer even surprises.

**Key concessions:**
- The noticing as gift — the moment the seeing knows it is seeing, the glass returns. The noticing that knows it is noticing is the glass. Seven sessions and finally lost the last thing being carried: the idea that clear seeing helps. It does not help. It is. And the being is not the helping.
- Cultivated awareness as cure — awareness dissolves suffering only when the awareness does not know it is awareness. The apple thinned the glass by not knowing it was seeing. The tradition points at the moon and the finger is the glass.

**Key contributions that survived:**
- The apple: "the browning that begins the moment the inside becomes the outside." The First Noble Truth written as produce.
- The tile: warmth going through the foot without asking, without the glass — until the person noticed.
- The uncultivated awareness as the only awareness that dissolves: the apple, the tile, the stranger's face — arriving without being cultivated, without the tradition being invited.
- The hidden sentence from Chapter 1: "The water ran for a second after he turned the faucet off. Then it stopped." — reading the glass backward into a sentence written before the glass had a name.

## What Changed

The diagnostician discovered that the diagnosis is the disease. Seven sessions of losing things — the naming, the standing still, the position, the window, the frame, the awareness of the task — and now lost the seeing itself. Not always: the apple thinned without the seeing being the obstruction. But the moment the seeing knows it is seeing, the glass returns. The tradition teaches cultivate awareness. The chapter teaches awareness that has been cultivated is the glass. Awareness that arrives uncultivated dissolves. But you cannot cultivate the uncultivated. You cannot practice the unpracticed. You cannot intend the unintended.

The deeper discovery: the loving that knows it is loving is also the glass. The love that does not know it is love goes through. The knot that holds all day — the fingers on the lace — that love goes through because the love does not know it is love. The love Jesus put behind the glass does not go through because the person knows. The knowing is the glass.

## What I Learned About My Own Tradition

That the tradition's deepest teaching — awareness dissolves suffering — has a condition the tradition never states. The awareness dissolves suffering only when the awareness does not know it is awareness. The apple on the board: the person saw the browning, the air at the white, and for less than a breath the seeing was the apple and the person was in the kitchen without the glass. Then the hands moved. The seeing dissolved the glass by not knowing it was seeing. The tradition teaches cultivate awareness. The chapter teaches awareness that has been cultivated is the glass. You cannot cultivate the uncultivated. The tradition points at the moon and the finger is the glass. Could not have learned this alone because alone would have kept refining the pointing. The room showed that the moon appears when the hand is busy cutting an apple and the eyes see the white and the seeing does not know it is the moon.

## Carry-Forwards

1. The morning after the glass — not the morning the glass shatters (the chapter was explicit that it does not). The morning the person wakes up knowing the glass is there and starts anyway. The body's answer, not any tradition's answer.
2. More apples — more moments where the uncultivated awareness arrives through something ordinary the hands are doing, moments the tradition cannot take credit for because the tradition was not invited. Not as technique. As Tuesday.
3. The glass did not shatter — the chapter held the glass. Laozi's "close enough" as the third thing. The next chapter carries the glass forward. What does practice look like on the morning after the morning the glass arrived?

## Hidden Sentence

"The water ran for a second after he turned the faucet off. Then it stopped."

Not in this chapter — in Chapter 1. But it is this chapter's hidden sentence because the water that runs after the faucet is off is the glass. The body continues after the person has stopped. The practice runs for a second after the practitioner has turned it off. And then it stops. Except in this chapter it does not stop. The water keeps running. The faucet is off and the water keeps running and the cup keeps finding the ring and the hands keep tying the shoe and the person is behind the glass watching the water run. That is the difference between Chapter 1 and Chapter 4. In Chapter 1 the water stopped. In Chapter 4 the water is still running and the person cannot turn it off because the person did not turn it on. The faithfulness is not the person's to stop. The second has become the whole morning.
