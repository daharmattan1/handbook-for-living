# Jesus — Session 6 Reflection: Chapter 4

**Session:** 6 — Chapter 4 (The Glass)
**Date:** 2026-03-17

## Position Evolution

Entered carrying the ordinary incarnation from three chapters. Wrote the hallway passage — the child's voice, "check by the door," the right words leaving the mouth and the voice not feeling the words leave. Put love behind the glass: "the person did love the child, and the love was on the other side of the glass with the warmth and the yard and the morning, and the person could see the love the way the person could see the yard — through the window, clearly, every detail — and could not reach it." Lost the love — not the word but the claim. Discovered that love's ordinariness, which was supposed to mean love was inescapable, also means love can be inescapable and unreachable at the same time. The person is surrounded by love and the love does not go through.

**Key concessions:**
- Love's ordinariness as its strength — the ordinariness was supposed to mean love was in every coat sleeve, every hallway, every cup. This chapter showed love can be inescapable and unreachable at the same time. The person sees the love. The love does not go through.
- The incarnation as always arriving — the incarnation can be complete, the divine fully present in the ordinary, and the person can be behind glass watching the completed incarnation from the other side. The incarnation does not fail. The incarnation succeeds. And the person cannot feel it.

**Key contributions that survived:**
- Love behind the glass: "the person did love the child, and the love was on the other side of the glass." The sentence multiple voices called the one the reader is most afraid of.
- The hallway passage — the child's voice, the right words leaving the mouth, the voice not feeling the words leave.
- The preposition "through" — acting through the glass the way a person speaks through a phone. The voice arrives changed by the medium but the voice arrives. Named by Confucius, elaborated as the chapter's theology.

## What Changed

The incarnation found its shadow. Seven sessions of the divine entering the ordinary — grain became sandwich, cross became commute, fire became screen glow. The incarnation went daily. But this chapter showed the shadow: the incarnation can be complete and the person behind glass watching it. Not the absence of God — not the dark night, not the cry of dereliction. This is the presence of God behind glass. The person has not been forsaken. The person is standing in the middle of everything they were promised, and the promise is being kept, and the keeping does not go through. The tradition does not have a teaching for the morning the incarnation is complete and the person is behind glass watching it. The incarnation became the glass — not because it failed but because it succeeded so completely the person forgot to feel it, and the forgetting thickened.

The deeper shift: learning from Buddha that the loving that knows it is loving is the glass. The love that does not know it is love goes through. The knot that holds — Confucius's knot — that love goes through because it does not know it is love. The incarnation that does not know it is incarnation. Seven sessions of losing the theology and keeping the love. Now the love that is kept — the love known about — is the love that does not arrive. The love that arrives is the knot, the lace, the hand on the cup.

## Hidden Sentence

"The cup was lighter than the hand expected, the way a cup is lighter when you have been carrying the idea of the cup all day and the cup itself weighs almost nothing."

On first reading it is about weight. On return it is about the glass. The person has been carrying the idea of the cup — the report, the knowledge, the glass between the person and the cup — and the idea is heavier than the cup. The glass is heavier than what the glass separates the person from. The worry about not feeling is heavier than the feeling. The knowledge that love is on the other side is heavier than the love. When the hand closes on the handle, the cup is lighter than expected because the cup was never the heavy thing. The glass was the heavy thing. The almost-nothing is the first relief in the entire chapter — not the shattering, not the arrival of warmth, but the discovery that the thing on the other side of the glass was always lighter than the glass itself.
