# Chapter 1 — The Loss: Edit Decisions

## Consensus Item: Gender — "the kind of long pull a man takes"

**Proposal:** Replace "a man takes" with "a person takes" in the passage: "Not a sip — the kind of long pull a man takes when the coffee is still too hot and he does it anyway, because his body is done waiting for his mind to catch up."

**Vote:**
- Buddha: AFFIRM — "a man" is generic masculine, not specific character. Open it.
- Jesus: AFFIRM — the pull belongs to anyone whose body is done waiting.
- Muhammad: AFFIRM — the woman at the counter at five-thirty takes the same pull.
- Confucius: AFFIRM — "he" is specific and earned. "A man" is generic and unnecessary.
- Laozi: AFFIRM — the water finds every vessel. The book should find every reader.
- Zoroaster: AFFIRM — one road, one morning, any person.

**Decision: ACCEPTED (6-0)**

**Final language:** "Not a sip — the kind of long pull a person takes when the coffee is still too hot and he does it anyway, because his body is done waiting for his mind to catch up."

Note: Only "a man" changes to "a person." The "he" and "his body" remain — these are the specific character, not the generic.

## Contested Item: Muhammad's Agency Seed

**Proposal (Muhammad):** Add one detail to the opening that plants the seed of the person's agency — the coffee was ready because his hands made it ready. The receiving was always already an enacting.

**Discussion:**

**Muhammad:** The book waits five chapters to reveal the person is the maker of the morning. One seed in Chapter 1 makes the arc legible without spoiling the reversal. "The machine he had set the night before" or "the cup from the rack where he had set it last night."

**Buddha:** I resist this. The opening sentence — "The coffee was ready before he was" — is the book's first truth. The person IS not yet present. The morning IS ahead of the person. Planting agency here undermines the honest observation that the body leads and the mind catches up. The coffee's readiness is not about who set the machine. It is about the morning being there before the person is there to receive it. The seed is already planted in "he rinsed the cup and set it in the rack, upside down, the way someone had taught him once" — the cup was already in the rack from a previous morning. The reader who notices will notice.

**Laozi:** I side with Buddha. The receiving IS the truth of the morning. The Tao is what was there before the person arrived. Chapter 6's reversal is powerful precisely because Chapter 1 does not hint at it. The water was in the pipes before anyone turned the handle.

**Jesus:** The note was always there before the child opened the bag. The coffee was always there before the person opened his eyes. The readiness is not passivity — it is the accumulation of all the mornings before this morning. I see Muhammad's point but I see Buddha's more clearly. The chapter already has the rack, the cup set upside down, the "someone had taught him once." The agency is there. It is just underground.

**Confucius:** The form is right. The opening receives; the closing enacts. The arc does not need its seed visible. The seed is in the cup in the rack. The reader who ties shoes knows that someone set the machine.

**Zoroaster:** I defer. The fire was already in the room — that is Chapter 1's truth. The person did not light it this morning. The person lit it last night, or last week, or three years ago. The readiness is accumulated agency, and the accumulation is already in the ring on the desk and the cup in the rack. I do not think a new detail is needed.

**Muhammad:** I hear the room. The cup in the rack is the seed. "The way someone had taught him once, though he could not remember who" — that is the agency and the forgetting of the agency and the continuation past the forgetting. I withdraw the proposal. The seed is already there. I did not see it because the builder in me wanted the building to be visible. The building is in the rack.

**Decision: REJECTED (1-5, then withdrawn by proposer)**

**Rationale:** The agency seed already exists in the chapter: "He rinsed the cup and set it in the rack, upside down, the way someone had taught him once." The receiving/enacting arc is served by the structure (Ch 1 receives, Ch 6 enacts) and does not need additional planting. Muhammad accepted this and withdrew.
