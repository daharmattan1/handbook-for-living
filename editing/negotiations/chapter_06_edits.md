# Chapter 6 — The Morning: Edit Decisions

## Proposal: The clearing risks reading as destination (Confucius)

**Discussion:**

**Confucius:** The arc of Chapters 1-6 implies progress. Chapter 6 feels like arrival — like the glass has been resolved. But the glass will return on Wednesday. The ring does not prefer the clearing. I propose one clause somewhere in Chapter 6 that acknowledges this morning is one morning among mornings.

**Buddha:** I support. The book's own logic says the thinning comes without cause. The reader carried through six chapters of increasing presence may believe they have been given a destination. One honest sentence says: this is a morning. The next morning may be glass.

**Laozi:** The final line already says it: "the clearing that did not know it was Tuesday." The clearing does not know it is the clearing. That IS the acknowledgment that it is not a destination. A destination knows it is a destination. The clearing does not. I am not sure an additional clause is needed.

**Muhammad:** I hear Laozi. The final line does the work. But the reader arrives at the final line after 400 lines of increasing warmth and agency. By the time they read "the clearing that did not know it was the clearing," they may already believe they have arrived. Confucius is asking for the seed BEFORE the final line, not a replacement for it.

**Zoroaster:** The reader inside the glass will read the arc as method — the person who keeps going through the morning arrives at the clearing. The book does not intend this, but the arc produces it. One sentence before the walk home — perhaps as the person stands at the gate after the child goes through — that says: the morning is the morning. Not the arrival. The morning.

**Jesus:** Grace cannot be kept. I learned this in the re-reading. The glass thinning for no reason cannot be stored or held or repeated on demand. I support one clause that carries this truth into Chapter 6. Not as a warning. As the simple fact that Muhammad's morning — the making of the coffee, the two spoonfuls, the walk to school — this morning will be followed by another morning that may be glass. And the ring will hold both mornings the same.

**Confucius:** Laozi is right that the final line does the work. But I want the work done before the reader arrives at the final line. My proposal: in the passage where the person walks back alone after dropping the child at school — "the same sidewalk, the same crack, the same tree root" — add one clause: "and the morning was a morning, and tomorrow would be a morning, and the ring would hold both of them."

**Laozi:** That is the ring's sentence, and the ring already holds both mornings in Chapter 4. Bringing the ring back here is unnecessary — the ring is already doing its work. But I will not block. If the room wants it, the ring can hold one more morning.

**Vote:**
- Confucius: AFFIRM (proposer)
- Buddha: AFFIRM
- Muhammad: AFFIRM
- Jesus: AFFIRM
- Zoroaster: AFFIRM
- Laozi: DEFER (not object — "the ring can hold one more morning")

**Decision: ACCEPTED (5-0, with Laozi deferring)**

**Action:** In the passage where the person walks back alone after the school drop-off, add a brief clause acknowledging this morning is one morning among mornings. Proposed language: somewhere in the return walk, "and the morning was the morning, and tomorrow would be a morning too, and neither of them would know which one the person would be present for."
