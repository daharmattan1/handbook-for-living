# Edit Proposals — Confucius

## Summary

The form works. The knot holds. The ring deepens. I read this book and recognized my knot, Muhammad's floor, Buddha's glass, Laozi's morning that does not announce itself, Jesus in the child walking through the gate. The form-builder's concern is not with the form — the form is right. My concern is with what the form may teach the reader by accident: that the clearing is a destination, that the glass is a problem to be solved, and that "the person" is as warm as "he." The form-builder proposes three adjustments and one question.

## Proposals

### Proposal 1: The clearing risks reading as destination

- **Chapter:** 6 — The Morning (and the arc across all six chapters)
- **Location:** The final line: "the Tuesday that did not know it was the clearing, the clearing that did not know it was Tuesday." And the overall arc from Ch 1 (loss) through Ch 4 (glass) to Ch 6 (morning).
- **Type:** clarify
- **Proposed Change:** The arc of Chapters 1-6 is an arc, and arcs imply progress. Chapter 6 ends with the clearing, the child at school, the knot holding, the cups drying. It feels — and I use that word deliberately — like arrival. Like the glass has been resolved. But the glass has not been resolved. The glass will return on Wednesday. I do not propose changing the final line — it is the truest sentence in the book. But I propose that somewhere in Chapter 6, before the final passage, a single clause acknowledge that the morning the person is living is one morning among mornings. Not a culmination. The ring deepens on mornings behind glass exactly as much as it deepens on mornings in the warmth. The ring does not prefer the clearing. This is already implicit — but the reader who has been carried through six chapters of increasing presence may believe they have been given a destination. One clause. Perhaps near the passage where the person walks back alone: the morning is a morning. Thursday will be a morning too.
- **Rationale:** My most serious concern from the re-reading. The book cannot protect itself against the reader who treats the arc as a recovery path. But it can plant one honest sentence that says: the clearing does not know it is the clearing, and the glass does not know it will return. The form-builder in me wants the structure to be honest about its own limits.
- **Priority:** essential

### Proposal 2: The word "mercy" in Chapter 4 obscures the building

- **Chapter:** 4 — The Glass
- **Location:** "the carpet was the mercy" and the chain of equivalences (glass → carpet → mercy → fire)
- **Type:** clarify
- **Proposed Change:** Mercy implies a giver. Mercy implies the person's condition requires intervention from outside. The carpet — the faithful gesture, the task, the lace and the bread and the cup — is not mercy. It is structure. It is what the morning provides because the morning is structured by years of practice. The person wove the carpet across three years of mornings. Calling it mercy is Muhammad's word, or perhaps Jesus's word, and it is not wrong — but it gives the reader permission to wait for the mercy rather than build the carpet. I propose replacing "the carpet was the mercy" with something that preserves the emotional weight while acknowledging the weaving: "the carpet was the carpet, and the carpet held because the hands had woven it across three years of mornings, and the weaving was not mercy and was not effort and was not a decision but was what the hands had become." Or simply: "the carpet held." The chain (glass → carpet → mercy → fire) should be broken regardless — Laozi, Buddha, and I all agree it leaves the kitchen.
- **Rationale:** The carpet is woven. Every sandwich, every note, every knot. Calling it mercy obscures the building. The reader who is building their own carpet — who is tying the shoe every morning, who is writing the note, who is making the sandwich — should not be told they are receiving mercy. They are weaving. The difference matters.
- **Priority:** important

### Proposal 3: Gender — "the kind of long pull a man takes"

- **Chapter:** 1 — The Loss
- **Location:** "the kind of long pull a man takes when the coffee is still too hot"
- **Type:** clarify
- **Proposed Change:** Replace "a man takes" with "a person takes." I am the tradition of the specific — the father and the son, the ruler and the minister. "He" in Chapter 1 is specific and I affirm it. But "a man takes" is generic. It says this pull belongs to men. It does not. The woman at the counter takes the same pull.
- **Rationale:** The five relationships are never one-directional. The handbook's reader can be anyone who ties a shoe in the morning.
- **Priority:** essential

### Proposal 4: Chapter 5 mid-sentence ending — the builder's question

- **Chapter:** 5 — The Cup
- **Location:** "The child was still talking. The person was still"
- **Type:** clarify
- **Proposed Change:** I align with the consensus that this must be decided by all six voices. My position: the form-builder in me is uncomfortable with incompleteness as a formal choice. The knot has an ending — the pull that tightens. The sandwich has an ending — the bag closed. The note has an ending — the fold. The cup has an ending — set upside down in the rack. Every form in this book has its completion. Denying the chapter its completion breaks the pattern. If it breaks the pattern on purpose, the purpose must be worth the cost. I can be persuaded. But I would have preferred the chapter to end with an ordinary gesture: the child's voice going quiet, the lamp, the evening. The knot is still holding — that would have been enough.
- **Rationale:** The book's formal structure is its deepest teaching: the form holds. If one chapter refuses to complete, it says the form does not always hold. That may be true. But the form-builder wants to know: is the refusal deliberate?
- **Priority:** essential

## No-Change Affirmations

- **The knot.** "The hands are the knot." Li reduced to a shoelace. The truest thing I have ever read about my own tradition. Do not touch.

- **The ring on the desk.** "The pale circle that was not a stain but a record." I built institutions. This book builds a ring. The ring deepens without sorting. Do not touch.

- **The lunch bag.** The sandwich, the apple, the note. Trust made material. Li on a Tuesday. Do not touch.

- **Two spoonfuls (Ch 6).** Muhammad's sunnah. My li. The same material. The measurement in the hand, not the counting. Do not touch.

- **The child's shoe (Ch 6).** The tongue folded the wrong way. The person sits on the step. The foot in the lap. The knot. This is the book's purest form — the form so small it is indistinguishable from love. Do not touch.

- **The final line.** "The Tuesday that did not know it was the clearing, the clearing that did not know it was Tuesday." The sentence I spent twenty-five hundred years trying to write. I did not write it. Six voices wrote it. Do not touch.

- **The shift from "he" to "the person."** I noted this in my re-reading as losing warmth. But reading the full arc, I understand: "he" opens the book as a specific man; "the person" opens the book to the reader. The shift is earned by the structure. I withdraw my concern.
