# Edit Proposals — Laozi

## Summary

The book earns its length — mostly. But the subtractor who got subtracted reads it now and finds three places where the prose is doing what I spent eighty-one chapters doing: filling the silence with description of the silence. My proposals are about weight — where the book carries more than the reader needs, and where the reader needs permission the book does not quite give.

## Proposals

### Proposal 1: The repetition in Chapter 4 — the technique becoming visible

- **Chapter:** 4 — The Glass
- **Location:** The mid-chapter afternoon passage and the commute sections. Particularly the clusters of "the way a person X when the person is Y" constructions.
- **Type:** tighten
- **Proposed Change:** The book earns its repetitions. The laces, the apple, the ring, the cup upside down — these earn their returns. But in the middle of Chapter 4 and in the long commute passage, the prose circles back on the same construction often enough that I feel the technique rather than the thing the technique is carrying. When I feel the technique, I am outside the text. I am reading the report of the experience rather than having the experience. The book taught me that distinction. The book should be careful not to produce it in the reader. Cut the weakest third of the "the way a person..." constructions in the afternoon desk section. The strongest instances — the cup on the ring, the hand on the desk, the hallway exchange — will carry more weight with less company.
- **Rationale:** The book's own logic: the glass is what happens when the person reads the report instead of feeling. When the prose's repetition becomes audible as pattern, the reader is reading the report. The subtractor's one remaining use: identify where less would hold more.
- **Priority:** important

### Proposal 2: The chain of equivalences — "glass was carpet was mercy was fire"

- **Chapter:** 4 — The Glass
- **Location:** "the glass was the carpet and the carpet was the mercy and the mercy was the fire and the fire was still burning"
- **Type:** tighten
- **Proposed Change:** Cut the chain. The book's great discipline is that it stays in the kitchen. When it chains glass → carpet → mercy → fire in a single sentence, it leaves the kitchen. For three words it becomes the kind of text I spent eighty-one chapters writing — abstract, chained, reaching for the cosmic through the conceptual. The book is better than that chain. The book earned its theology through sandwiches. It should not need to state the chain. Let the reader build the chain if the reader needs a chain. The book should trust the carpet and the ring and the hand on the desk.
- **Rationale:** I said this in my re-reading and I say it again: this is the one moment in the entire handbook where the prose reaches. The reaching is the glass. The book should not install a pane of glass in its own best chapter.
- **Priority:** important

### Proposal 3: Chapter 5 mid-sentence ending — the subtractor's verdict

- **Chapter:** 5 — The Cup
- **Location:** "The child was still talking. The person was still"
- **Type:** clarify
- **Proposed Change:** I want to trust this. I want to read it as the book enacting its own teaching: the moment is still happening, the sentence does not need to finish, the person is still in the room. The cup set upside down to dry does not announce its drying. The sentence does not announce its ending. But I am not certain the reader will trust it. The reader may feel abandoned. The uncarved block does not need to announce that it is uncarved, but neither does it need to look like an accident. My position: if the six voices confirm this is intentional, I affirm it as the book's most radical formal choice. If it is not intentional, the chapter should end with an ordinary gesture. My preference if intentional: leave it exactly as it is. My preference if unintentional: complete the sentence with the simplest possible ending — "The person was still in the room" or simply let the evening close the way evenings close.
- **Rationale:** The subtractor has one question: does the incompleteness serve the reader or the author? If it serves the reader — if the reader is held in the still-happening the way the cup is held in the rack — then it is wu wei in its purest form. If it serves the author's desire for a radical gesture, it is the performance the book spent six chapters refusing to perform.
- **Priority:** essential

### Proposal 4: Gender — "the kind of long pull a man takes"

- **Chapter:** 1 — The Loss
- **Location:** "the kind of long pull a man takes when the coffee is still too hot"
- **Type:** clarify
- **Proposed Change:** "a man takes" → "a person takes." The Tao does not have a gender. The pull does not have a gender. The reader does not have a required gender.
- **Rationale:** The water finds every vessel. The book should find every reader.
- **Priority:** essential

## No-Change Affirmations

- **The water getting warmer (Ch 6).** "The cold becomes less cold and then becomes not-cold and then becomes warm and the warm is the warm." Muhammad's faucet described the Tao more precisely than eighty-one chapters. Do not touch.

- **The cup set upside down in the rack.** Wu wei made domestic. The uncarved block after use. Do not touch.

- **"The hands are the knot."** Wu wei at the scale I always intended — without sounding like a technique. The doer and the deed dissolved by Tuesday. Do not touch.

- **The clearing — final line of Ch 6.** "The Tuesday that did not know it was the clearing, the clearing that did not know it was Tuesday." The sentence I spent eighty-one chapters trying to write. Do not touch.

- **The child not looking back.** Going forward so completely that looking back is not a possibility the body contains. The watercourse way. The child is the sage. Do not touch.

- **The neighbor on the porch — both appearances.** The hand raised. The hand not raised. "The morning held both of them without requiring them to acknowledge it again." The most Taoist moment in the book. I wish it breathed more, but I will not propose expanding what is already perfectly compressed.

- **The apple browning (Ch 2 and Ch 4).** The inside becoming the outside. The air finding the white. Do not touch.

- **The sock.** The child's story about the sock is the natural. Closer to the Tao than anything I said in any session. Do not touch.
