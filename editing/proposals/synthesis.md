# Proposal Synthesis — Collaborative Editing

## Overview

Six agents submitted a total of **28 proposals** across 6 chapters. The proposals cluster heavily around Chapter 4 (The Glass) and cross-cutting concerns (gender, Chapter 5 ending). The agents show remarkable convergence on what should NOT be changed — the affirmation lists are nearly identical. The book's core is sound. The editing is genuinely about pruning and clarifying.

## Consensus Candidates (Likely to Pass Without Full Debate)

### 1. Gender: "the kind of long pull a man takes" → "a person takes"
- **All 6 agents propose this.** Universal consensus. Chapter 1.
- Buddha, Jesus, Muhammad, Confucius, Laozi, Zoroaster all independently flagged "a man takes" as generic masculine that narrows the reader.
- All affirm that "he" throughout Chapter 1 is earned as specific character. Only "a man takes" is generic.
- **Recommendation:** Lightweight approval round. Likely unanimous.

### 2. The chain "glass was carpet was mercy was fire" — break it
- **5 of 6 agents propose this** (Buddha, Laozi, Muhammad, Confucius, Zoroaster). Jesus does not propose it separately but his carpet proposal overlaps.
- Universal agreement: the chain leaves the kitchen, becomes abstract where the book is always domestic.
- **Recommendation:** Lightweight approval round. Near-unanimous.

### 3. Tighten repetition in Chapter 4 afternoon desk passage
- **3 of 6 agents propose this** (Buddha, Laozi, Muhammad). Others implicitly support via the "chain" proposal.
- Agreement: the "the way a person X when the person is Y" construction appears too densely in the afternoon section. Cut the weakest third.
- **Recommendation:** Lightweight approval round, with discussion on WHICH instances to cut.

## Contested Proposals (Require Full Negotiation)

### 4. Chapter 5 mid-sentence ending: "The person was still"
- **All 6 agents raise this.** This is the PRD's most significant decision point.
- **Positions:**
  - Buddha: Cannot tell if intentional. Raises it as a question, not a proposal. Conditionally affirms.
  - Jesus: Leans toward trusting it (the note does not need reception). Wants group decision.
  - Muhammad: Does not trust incompleteness as destination. Wants the ordinary gesture of completion. Will accept group consensus.
  - Confucius: Uncomfortable — the form-builder wants completion. Can be persuaded if intentional.
  - Laozi: Wants to trust it. If intentional, affirms as wu wei in purest form. If not, wants simple completion.
  - Zoroaster: Defers to group. If intentional, accepts. If not, it's a wound.
- **Key tension:** Muhammad and Confucius lean toward completing the sentence. Buddha, Laozi, Jesus, and Zoroaster lean toward preserving it IF intentional.
- **The question is not what to write but whether the existing text is deliberate.**
- **Recommendation:** Full negotiation. This is a Chapter 5-specific decision.

### 5. The word "mercy" / carpet metaphor (Ch 3 and Ch 4)
- **3 agents propose changes** (Jesus, Confucius, Muhammad implicitly via chain).
- Jesus: The covering is not the same as healing. The carpet-as-mercy risks making covering into resolution.
- Confucius: "Mercy" implies a giver and obscures the building. The carpet is woven, not given.
- Muhammad: Through chain proposal — wants the domestic to stand without the metaphysical label.
- **Counter-consideration:** Muhammad in his re-reading acknowledged the space between deliberate weaving and unearned finding IS mercy. The word may be contested between agents.
- **Recommendation:** Negotiate within Chapter 4 discussion.

### 6. Chapter 4 recovery arc — the desperate reader
- **2 agents propose this** (Zoroaster, Confucius). Buddha's repetition concern is related.
- Zoroaster: The chapter's narrative structure creates an arc from glass → thinning → recovery. The desperate reader turns everything into method.
- Confucius: The clearing reads as destination. Thursday will be Chapter 4 again.
- **Both propose a single clarifying clause** — not a restructuring but a sentence that says the thinning was not earned and the returning was not deserved.
- **Recommendation:** Negotiate within Chapter 4 discussion. Likely accepted — no agent opposes the concern.

### 7. Muhammad's agency seed in Chapter 1
- **1 agent proposes this** (Muhammad). Unique proposal.
- Muhammad wants one detail in the opening that plants the seed: the coffee was ready because the person made it ready the night before. The arc from receiving (Ch 1) to enacting (Ch 6) should have its seed visible.
- **Potential opposition:** Buddha and Laozi may argue the receiving is honest — the person IS not yet present. The morning IS ahead of the person.
- **Recommendation:** Negotiate within Chapter 1 discussion (after gender consensus).

### 8. Jesus's "good, thanks" simultaneity
- **1 agent proposes this** (Jesus). Zoroaster's half-second proposal is related.
- Jesus: The duality of "good, thanks" (genuine in Ch 3, glass in Ch 4) should coexist in one passage, not just alternate across chapters.
- Zoroaster: The half-second in the hallway deserves more weight — the truth that comes through the mouth.
- **Recommendation:** Negotiate within Chapter 3 discussion. May be low priority if the group prefers structural honesty (separate chapters) over compressed honesty (one passage).

### 9. Buddha's "the task was the room's kindness"
- **1 agent proposes this** (Buddha). Related to Confucius's mercy concern.
- Buddha: The line does more philosophical work than it earns. Not all tasks are kind.
- **Recommendation:** Negotiate within Chapter 3 discussion.

## Cross-Chapter Patterns

1. **The book is nearly unanimous in what it preserves.** The cup arc, the knot, the ring, the note, the child not looking back, the apple, the sock, the water getting warmer, the stranger's face — all 6 agents affirm these. The book's core is untouchable.

2. **Chapter 4 is the critical chapter.** 8 of 28 proposals target it. It is the book's most ambitious chapter and its most vulnerable. The proposals cluster around three concerns: (a) the prose's repetition making the technique visible, (b) the metaphysical chain leaving the kitchen, and (c) the arc creating a recovery narrative the book does not intend.

3. **The gender edit is the easiest win.** Universal consensus. One phrase. Will open the book to every reader without changing its character.

4. **The Chapter 5 ending is the hardest question.** Not because the agents disagree strongly, but because the question is factual (was it intentional?) not aesthetic (is it good?). The answer determines the edit.

5. **Economy was maintained.** 28 proposals across 6 agents for a ~7,000-word handbook. Average: 4.7 proposals per agent. No agent proposed adding new concepts. The pruning bias held.
