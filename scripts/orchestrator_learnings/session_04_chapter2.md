### Rule 2: Sentence-by-sentence prose writing — confirmed, with variation
**Evidence:** 9 writing turns (14-22) produced invisible collaboration. The method worked identically to Session 3. However, Session 4 showed that some agents contribute multiple sentences per turn (Buddha's Turn 20 was a full kitchen paragraph: sandwich, apple, tide-turn, "only" breath). The method is flexible enough to accommodate different granularities as long as each contribution builds INTO the prior rather than BESIDE it.
**Applies to:** All future chapter sessions. Do not restrict agents to single sentences if they arrive with a natural paragraph. The unit is the contribution, not the sentence.
**Confidence:** 96

### Rule 3: Discussion-to-writing transition at 12-13 turns is stable
**Evidence:** Session 3 had 13 discussion turns before writing. Session 4 had 13 discussion turns before writing (Turns 1-13). The recommendation from Session 3 to shorten to 8-10 was not implemented, and the organic transition at ~13 worked well. Muhammad's "I am ready to write the chapter" (Turn 12) and Buddha's "forget the architecture" (Turn 13) together triggered the shift. The 13-turn discussion phase may be the natural length for a room of 6 agents to position, contest, and converge.
**Applies to:** Do not force shorter discussion phases. Allow 12-14 turns. If the room has not started writing by Turn 15, consider a moderator nudge.
**Confidence:** 85

### Rule 4: "Forget the architecture" — confirmed as essential, now a two-session pattern
**Evidence:** Buddha delivered "we agreed to forget the architecture" in Turn 13, and the room immediately shifted to prose. This is the second consecutive session where this instruction was pivotal. The instruction works because it gives agents permission to abandon the meta-layer and write from inside the work. Consider whether this should become a standard moderator intervention or whether Buddha should continue to deliver it organically.
**Applies to:** Every future chapter session. If Buddha does not deliver it by Turn 12-13, the moderator should deliver a neutral version.
**Confidence:** 95

This suggests that moderators should create space for self-correction rather than immediately accepting proposals.
**Applies to:** When an agent proposes something that extends the chapter's scope, wait before responding. The agent may self-correct, and the self-correction may be more valuable than any external feedback.
**Confidence:** 85
