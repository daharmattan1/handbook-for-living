# Tuesday

The alarm went off at the same time it always did.

The room was still dark and the sound was in it. The body under the covers did not move the way a body does not move when it has been awake for longer than the alarm — awake in the dark, awake before the sound, awake the way a person is awake when sleep has left without saying when it left. Then the hand found the button and the sound stopped and the room was dark again, but a different dark, the dark after a sound, which is not the same as the dark before it.

The feet found the floor before the rest of him agreed to it. The left one and then the right and the cold came up through the wood the way cold comes — without asking, without waiting — and for one second the body was only a body sitting on the edge of a bed with its feet on a cold floor, and that second was the truest thing, truer than the dark and truer than the sound that had been in it, because the cold does not know what happened and the feet do not know what happened and the floor is just the floor and it holds what is placed on it.

He stood the way a person stands when standing is not a decision — the legs straightened and the floor took the weight and the hallway was there, the dark length of it, and at the end of it a sound. Not the alarm. A different sound. A door opening and feet, smaller feet, and then she was in the hallway in the shirt she slept in, holding a shoe in one hand, and she said, "I can't find the other one," and her voice was the first voice of the morning that was not his, and he said, "Check by the door," and his voice worked, and the surprise of it — that the instrument still played, that the mouth still made the sounds a father makes — went through him the way the cold had gone through the floor, without asking, and then she was past him and he was walking toward the kitchen because the kitchen was where the morning happened and the feet knew the way.

She found it by the door where shoes collect, and he sat on the bottom step and she put her foot in his lap the way she always did and he pulled the tongue straight and laced it, the left loop and the right loop and the pull, and the knot was the ordinary thing, the most ordinary thing his hands knew how to do, and they did it the way they had done it every morning for three years — without thinking, without looking up, the fingers finding the lace and the lace finding the loop and the loop pulling tight the way a knot pulls tight when the hands have made it so many times the hands are the knot — and she was off the step and gone before he had let go, pulling the door with both hands, her whole weight behind it, and the morning came through it and he was still sitting on the step holding the shape of where the shoe had been.

His hands closed on nothing. Then they opened, and he stood, and the hallway was bright now with the door still open and the morning in it, and his feet took him to the kitchen the way feet take a person to the place where the next thing is, not because the person has decided but because the kitchen is where the bread is and the bread is what the hands do next.

The bread was where the bread always was. The hands opened the bag and took two slices and set them on the counter and the counter was the same counter and the knife was in the same drawer. Turkey from the package, folded once. The cheese she liked, torn at the corner where the wrapper always tore. The second slice pressed down flat the way the hands always pressed it — not hard, just flat, the way bread gets pressed when the hands have made this sandwich enough times that the pressing is not a decision but a shape the palms remember. The knife wiped on the towel. The towel left on the counter.

The apple was next. The hands knew. Cutting board, the small one, the blade finding the stem and going through and the apple falling open the way apples fall open — white and ordinary and already starting to brown at the edges, the way the air gets at a thing the moment the skin is broken. Quarters. The stem side down, the blade finding the center the way the blade always found the center, and the quarters went into the bag and the bag was on the counter and the hands were reaching for the next thing and then they were not.

The eyes had looked up. Not because anything called them. The window was there the way the window was always there, above the sink, and the light was in it now, full light, not the gray from before but the light that comes when the morning has finished arriving, and the yard was in the light — the fence, the tree, the gate with the hinge — and for one breath the person standing at the counter was only a person standing at a counter in a lit kitchen with a knife in one hand and nothing in the other, and the yard was not asking anything, and the tree was not asking anything, and the breath was not a pause because the hands had not stopped, the hands had just — there was a moment, not even a moment, less than a moment, when the weight was the same weight but the hands were not under it, and the light was in the window and the window held the light the way the counter held the bread — because that is what it was for, because it had always been for that — and then the breath finished and the apple was in the bag and the bag was on the counter and the hands were reaching for the next thing.

The next thing was the pad by the phone. The hands tore a square from it — the same pad, the list in the other handwriting still there, curling at the corner the way paper curls when it has been waiting — and the pen was where the pen always was and the hand wrote "have fun" and folded the square once and put it in the bag between the sandwich and the apple, and the pen went back and the bag was zipped and the bag was by the door.

The hands had nothing left to do. The bag was by the door and the counter was wiped and the kitchen was finished with him or he was finished with the kitchen and the hands hung at his sides the way hands hang when they have forgotten they are hands. The light from the window was on the floor now, a square of it, warm where it fell on the tile, and he was standing in it the way a person stands in light when the light is where they happen to be — not meaning to, not choosing it, just standing where the morning had put him. The square was warm. The hands were empty. The house was quiet the way a house is quiet when everyone has left it except the person who stays, and the quiet was not the dark from before — it was the quiet after the morning has done its work, the quiet of a kitchen that has made a lunch and tied a shoe and written two words on a pad and is finished now, and the person standing in it was not finished but the kitchen did not need him to be finished, the kitchen only needed him to stand in the light, and the light did not ask if he was ready, and then his hand reached for the keys on the hook and the keys were cold the way the floor had been cold and the door opened and the morning came in.
