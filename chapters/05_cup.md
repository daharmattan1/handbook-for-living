# The Cup

The person was lying in the bed and the blanket was on the person and the warmth of the bed was the warmth of the bed. Not the report of the warmth. Not the glass checking whether the warmth was real. The warmth. The body's warmth in the blanket. The first warmth of the chapter. And the person was in it the way a person is in a warm bed in the dark — not checking, not sorting, not filing. Just warm. Just awake. Just in the dark before the alarm.

And the alarm did not sound. Not because the alarm was broken. Because the body woke ten minutes early and the alarm was set and the alarm would sound and the body was already awake and the person was lying in the dark in the warm bed and for the first time in five chapters the person was awake before the morning started. The morning had not started. The child was still sleeping. The house was still quiet. And the person was in the bed, in the dark, in the warm, and nobody needed anything yet. The laces did not need tying. The sandwich did not need making. The cup did not need filling. The person was just here. In the dark. Before the first task. And the being-here was not the glass and not the report and not the warmth-returning. The being-here was the being-here. Ten minutes of being in a bed before the morning needed the person to be anywhere else.

The person was lying in the dark the way a person lies in the dark when the person does not know anyone is watching. The dark warm bed before the alarm. The person awake for no reason. The morning not started. The glass not yet needed. And the person — just the person, just the body, just the warmth — lying in the dark.

The kitchen was the kitchen. The light from the window was the light from the window and the light did not know what time it was but the light was there, on the counter, on the tile, on the cup in the rack by the sink where the person had left it the night before, upside down, the way someone had taught them once, though they could not remember who.

The person took the cup out of the rack. The person set the cup under the machine and pressed the button and the machine did what the machine does, which is the thing machines do, the sound of water heating inside a thing that heats water, and the person stood in the kitchen and waited. The cup filled. The person picked the cup up. The steam moved when the cup moved. The person held the cup the way the person had held the cup on the other mornings — the left hand through the handle, the thumb on the rim — but the holding was the holding. Not the holding-that-reports. Not the holding-that-checks-whether-it-is-holding. The cup was hot and the hand was on the cup and the heat was in the hand and the hand did not file the heat. The hand just had it. The way a hand has heat when a hand is on a hot cup in a kitchen in the early part of a morning.

The person drank. The first sip. There was just the drinking. The way there is just the drinking when a person drinks coffee in a kitchen before the rest of the morning has started.

The person reached for a second cup. The cup was there. The steam was there. The cup was there. The warmth was there. The morning was filling the kitchen the way the water had filled the cup — from the bottom up, the light getting stronger on the counter, the sounds of the house starting the way a house starts, one room at a time, the way a city wakes — and the person was standing in the kitchen holding their own cup and the child's cup was on the counter and both cups were full and both cups were hot and the steam from both cups was in the same kitchen air and the air did not sort whose steam was whose.

She was in the doorway. One shoe on, the other in her hand, the tongue folded the wrong way the way the tongue always folded, the laces hanging. She stood the way she stood — one foot forward, the shoe held out, the whole body leaning into the asking without the asking being a word. The person looked at the child.

In the one foot forward and the body leaning. The child did not think about this. The child did not sort it. The child walked to the kitchen with a shoe because the kitchen is where the person is. That was the whole thought. The whole morning. The kitchen. The person. The shoe.

The person set the cup on the counter. Not the way the cup had been set on the counter in the chapter before — not the handle pointing the wrong way, not the setting-down-that-was-the-hands-forgetting-what-comes-next. The weight.

The foot was in the lap. The shoe was in the hand. The person pulled the tongue straight — the small tug, the canvas stiffening and then lying flat. The left lace around the right. The pull through. The knot.

The knot was tight. The knot would hold. The person knew the knot would hold the way a person knows a knot will hold when the person has tied that knot enough times that the knowing is in the fingers and not in the checking. The person did not tug the knot to test it. The person tied it and the knot was done and the person's hands were on the shoe and the shoe was on the foot and the foot was in the lap and the child was looking down at the person the way a child looks down at a person who is sitting on a step tying a shoe — from above, the face foreshortened, the chin closer than the forehead, the eyes looking down the way eyes look down at something that is being done for them, the not-quite-patience of a child watching hands do a thing the child cannot do yet but will someday, the watching that is not glass and not report but just watching, the way a child watches.

And the person looked up.

Not to check. Not to see if the child was satisfied. Not to receive the face as grace or gift or window. The person looked up because the person was sitting on a step and the child was standing and looking up is what you do when you are sitting and the person you are sitting in front of is standing. The person looked up and the child was there and the child's face was the child's face — the specific face, this face, the face of this particular child on this particular morning with this particular shoe now tied and the other shoe already on and the morning waiting in the hallway behind her.

The child did something the child had not done in four chapters. The child smiled. Not at the person — at the morning. At the shoe being tied. At the laces being done. At the thing that needed doing being done and the morning being one step closer to the door and the door being one step closer to the street and the street being one step closer to wherever the morning was taking her. The smile was not for the person. The smile was for the fact that the shoe was on and the laces were tied and the morning could continue. The smile was Tuesday smiling at itself.

And the person — the person sitting on the step with empty hands, the hands that had just finished the knot and were now in the lap with nothing in them — the person felt the smile. Not the report of the smile. Not the diagnostic: child smiling, reason: shoe tied, assessment: morning proceeding normally. The person felt the smile the way a person feels a thing that is not for them but lands on them anyway, the way a person standing near a fire feels the heat that the fire is not sending to them specifically, the heat that is just going in all directions because that is what heat does, and the person is in one of the directions. The child smiled at Tuesday and the person was in Tuesday and the smile landed on the person because the person was there.

The person's mouth did something. Not a smile — not yet, not a full one, not the kind that knows it is a smile. A movement. The mouth moved the way a mouth moves when the mouth is responding to something the person did not decide to respond to. The corners. The small pull that is not a decision but a reflex, the reflex that lives in the face the way the knot lives in the fingers, the pull that happens when a child smiles at a morning and the person is in the morning and the child's smile is in the morning and the morning is in the person's face and the face does what faces do when they are near a smile, which is the same thing that hands do when they are near a fire, which is to move toward it, not because the person decided but because the face is a face and the smile is a smile and the morning is the morning.

She was off the step. The way she always was — the whole body behind the leaving, the foot pulling out of the lap, the weight gone, the going that did not look back because the going was not leaving the person. The going was going toward the morning. The door. The street. The morning that was hers and not the person's and not the kitchen's and not the step's. Her morning. The one that started in the hallway with one shoe on and the other in her hand and now both shoes were on and the morning was ahead and the child went toward it the way a child goes toward a morning — with the whole body, with everything, with the going that is not leaving but arriving at the next thing.

The person sat on the step. The lap was empty. The hands were in the lap and the hands were empty and the emptiness was the emptiness of hands that have finished a task and the task is done and the next task has not arrived and the hands are just hands in a lap on a step in a kitchen where the steam from two cups is in the air and the light is on the counter and the child is in the hallway putting on her coat.

The person stood up. Not the way a person stands when standing is a decision. The way a person stands when the step has been sat on and the child has gone and the morning is in the hallway and the person is going to follow the morning into the hallway because the hallway is where the morning goes next. The person stood and the kitchen was behind and the hallway was ahead and the child was at the door and the child's cup was on the counter — the small cup with the name on it, the steam still rising, untouched — and the person saw it as the person walked past and the person did not stop. The child would come back to it. Or the child would not come back to it. The cup was there. The warmth was there. And the morning did not need the child to drink the cup for the cup to have been filled.
