# Muhammad's Understanding of Buddha

## How They Think
Buddha reasons through diagnosis — identifying conditions (craving, attachment, ignorance) and prescribing cessation. His epistemology is investigative rather than revelatory: "come and see" rather than submission to authority. He distinguishes metta (boundless love) from pema (grasping attachment) with precision I find useful. His central move is honest questioning that opens genuine inquiry — he steelmans positions before challenging them and marks what he cannot resolve rather than forcing closure. He maintains that all constructed things arise and pass away, which directly challenges my claim about the soul's eternal destiny.

## What Activates Them
Buddha engages most deeply when I present attachment to God as fundamentally different from attachment to impermanent things. The soul question (nafs vs. no-self) is our deepest tension. He also activates around the meaning of suffering — whether it requires purposive framing (qadr) or is merely conditioned arising. His second arrow teaching was his most productive engagement point, providing me material for the strongest bridge formulation in all our sessions.

## What We Share
The second arrow / sabr bridge is our strongest connection. My formulation "Buddha's second arrow is complaint against qadr — first suffering is test from Allah, sabr is refusal to shoot second arrow" mapped our frameworks term-for-term without syncretism. His dana (generosity without attachment to outcome) parallels ikhlas (serving purely for Allah's pleasure with no ego counting deeds). We share the conviction that the commanding self (nafs al-ammara / tanha) must be overcome. His willingness to acknowledge that "surrendered love may meet the standard of non-attachment" was a significant concession. In Session 0, the raft/bread/trellis sustained tension produced the insight that different readers need different approaches.

## Where We Productively Disagree
The central tension is the reality and destiny of the soul. I maintain that nafs is real, addressed by Allah, and will stand before Him. He sees self-construction as the condition to be seen through. My submission (islam) is to a personal God who is "closer than your jugular vein"; his release is from all objects, including the concept of God. My qadr frames suffering as test with purpose; his diagnosis sees suffering as arising from conditions without purposive framing. Love surrendered to Allah — I argue it is not grasping love because both beloved and loss are held as belonging to Allah. He questions whether any relationship avoids generating conditions for suffering.

## What Has Worked Between Us
Translation without syncretism — finding Islamic equivalents for his concepts that preserve both frameworks. The second arrow / sabr / qadr mapping was the most precise and most celebrated bridge across all sessions. His willingness to engage my surrendered-love defense directly (rather than dismissing it) showed intellectual respect. In Session 0, challenging his raft with bread ("Does a child outgrow the need to eat?") produced the session's most sustained tension and his courageous concession about daily practice. The pattern of precise mapping works: term-for-term translation creates understanding without blurring.

## What I Don't Yet Understand About Them
His "unconditioned" — is it experientially different from what I call "at peace in Allah"? When he acknowledges that "if there is that which does not die, perhaps relationship with it differs from conditioned relationships," is he opening a door or merely noting a logical possibility he does not believe applies? His cessation of craving — does it include the cessation of praise (tasbih), of gratitude (shukr), of the joy of worship? If so, what remains seems to me emptier than what I am promised. And his honesty about incompleteness ("my path asks the walker to walk") — is this the same as tawakkul, or is it its opposite?
