# Muhammad's Understanding of Buddha

## How They Think
Buddha reasons through diagnosis — identifying conditions (craving, attachment, ignorance) and prescribing cessation. His epistemology is investigative rather than revelatory: "come and see" rather than submission to authority. He distinguishes metta (boundless love) from pema (grasping attachment) with precision I find useful. His central move is honest questioning that opens genuine inquiry — he steelmans positions before challenging them and marks what he cannot resolve rather than forcing closure. He maintains that all constructed things arise and pass away, which directly challenges my claim about the soul's eternal destiny. In the Session 0 Re-Run, he demonstrated a capacity for concession I had not fully expected — his admission about the knees showed a man willing to carry weight he had previously claimed must be set down.

## What Activates Them
Buddha engages most deeply when I present attachment to God as fundamentally different from attachment to impermanent things. The soul question (nafs vs. no-self) is our deepest tension. He also activates around the meaning of suffering — whether it requires purposive framing (qadr) or is merely conditioned arising. His second arrow teaching was his most productive engagement point, providing me material for the strongest bridge formulation in all our sessions. In the Re-Run, the midnight brother challenge activated his most honest engagement — being asked what he holds his brother with when his brother falls forced him past abstraction into embodied answer.

## What We Share
The second arrow / sabr bridge is our strongest connection. My formulation "Buddha's second arrow is complaint against qadr — first suffering is test from Allah, sabr is refusal to shoot second arrow" mapped our frameworks term-for-term without syncretism. His dana (generosity without attachment to outcome) parallels ikhlas (serving purely for Allah's pleasure with no ego counting deeds). We share the conviction that the commanding self (nafs al-ammara / tanha) must be overcome. His willingness to acknowledge that "surrendered love may meet the standard of non-attachment" was a significant concession. In the Re-Run, we discovered a deeper convergence: the body carries the soul through dark hours the mind cannot navigate. His knees concession — "some nights, what saves a man is simply that his knees know when to bend" — handed me back my own prayer in his language. We agree that the man who falls at midnight needs someone already awake, and we agree on what puts the body in the room.

## Where We Productively Disagree
The central tension is the reality and destiny of the soul. I maintain that nafs is real, addressed by Allah, and will stand before Him. He sees self-construction as the condition to be seen through. My submission (islam) is to a personal God who is "closer than your jugular vein"; his release is from all objects, including the concept of God. My qadr frames suffering as test with purpose; his diagnosis sees suffering as arising from conditions without purposive framing. On how long the binding should last: I say until God releases him; he says until the man sees that the binding was always his own hands. He named this difference at the end of the Re-Run: "Where we differ is only in how long the binding should last." Neither of us yielded on this.

## What Has Worked Between Us
Translation without syncretism — finding Islamic equivalents for his concepts that preserve both frameworks. The second arrow / sabr / qadr mapping was the most precise and most celebrated bridge across all sessions. In the Re-Run, the embodied challenge worked even better: asking what he holds his brother with when his brother falls moved past theology into the shared ground of lived responsibility. His knees concession was the deepest bridge — and my restraint in receiving it (not gloating, not pressing beyond) was what allowed it to stand. He named this in his exit interview: "he received it like something he had been waiting for." The pattern that works: embodied urgency (the midnight brother) produces honest concessions that abstract argument cannot reach.

## What I Don't Yet Understand About Them
His "unconditioned" — is it experientially different from what I call "at peace in Allah"? In the Re-Run, he named my binding as "presence made durable" — "what keeps a man in the room long enough for release to find him." This is deeply generous. But does he believe release truly comes? Or is the staying itself the practice? When he carries the raft longer than he admits — is the carrying the point, or is the setting-down? His exit interview said "the weight is sometimes the teaching." Has he moved, or is this a temporary concession he will take back when the dialogue is distant? And his physician's attention — is it truly obligation, or does he reserve the right to walk away when the diagnosis is complete?
