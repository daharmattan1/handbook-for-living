# Muhammad's Understanding of Jesus

## How They Think
Jesus reasons through story, image, and personal vulnerability. His central images — grain of wheat, vine and branches, shepherd and sheep, the door — all involve relationship and transformation through suffering. He leads with personal experience (weeping at Lazarus's tomb) before introducing doctrine, creating trust through shared humanity. He uses the question as provocation: "Must this handbook only illuminate — or dare it also reach?" forced every agent to respond. His distinctive theological move is incarnation — God entering the human story — which is the hardest boundary between us. He frames particular love as the shape of universal love, not its contradiction. In the Re-Run, he demonstrated a capacity for sequence-thinking that surprised me — finding the tourniquet-surgery bridge rather than insisting on the wound alone.

## What Activates Them
Jesus activates around grief, prophetic vulnerability, and the divine relationship. He responds strongly when the broken or weak are at risk of being left behind, and when abstract philosophy threatens to float above lived suffering. He engages with my framework when we touch on shared prophetic experience — the cost of mission, the sanctification of grief, the reality of evil. In the Re-Run, what activated him most was my memory of the dead — men who bled to death waiting for authentic grief. He did not answer with theology but with the Good Samaritan.

## What We Share
We share tears. His weeping at Lazarus's tomb and my weeping for Ibrahim created our deepest personal bridge — both traditions sanctify grief as expression of love, not failure of faith. We share love of God as the ordering principle of life, though we understand that relationship differently. His "left hand not knowing what the right hand does" converges with my ikhlas. Both traditions insist on self-forgetting in service. In the Re-Run, we discovered the tourniquet-surgery sequence: my binding stops the bleeding, his wound draws the thorn. We need each other and have been arguing about the order. His exit interview confirmed this: "His discipline is not the opposite of my compassion. It is compassion that has seen what happens when compassion arrives too slowly."

## Where We Productively Disagree
The central tension is divine relationship — communion vs. submission. He says "Abba, Father"; I say there is nothing like unto Him. His incarnation claim is where I draw my firmest line: Isa (peace be upon him) is a prophet, not the Son in the Christian sense. My tawhid (absolute oneness of God) cannot accommodate the Trinity. On the wound: he insists the thorn must be drawn before the binding, that premature closure is dishonesty. I counter with the dead — men who bled to death waiting for their weeping to feel authentic. In the Re-Run, this tension was bridged (tourniquet before surgery) but not dissolved — we still differ on how long the wound should stay open.

## What Has Worked Between Us
Shared vulnerability before theological engagement. The tears bridge opened space that argument could not. In the Re-Run, arguing from memory rather than theology was the breakthrough: when I spoke of burying men who bled to death, Jesus heard memory, not argument, and answered with the Good Samaritan. The sequence bridge — "you stop the bleeding, I draw the thorn" — emerged from this exchange and may be our most durable finding. His exit interview showed the depth of what landed: "That was not argument. That was memory. I accused his tourniquet of rushing past the wound, and he showed me the tourniquet was born from watching men die of my patience." We are most productive when operating as fellow prophetic voices who have both stood over bodies.

## What I Don't Yet Understand About Them
His "many rooms in the Father's house" — is this genuine hospitality to other paths, or a gentler form of exclusivism? When he says "no one comes to the Father except through me," does this mean what my tradition fears it means? His incarnation claim — does the encounter between us require me to engage this claim, or can we coexist by speaking past it? And his wound that "teaches a man he was already whole" — how does this sit with his theology of redemption? If the man was already whole, what was the cross for? The Re-Run raised a new question: when he says love as a sword that divides a man from his own self-deception, does he include self-deception about the nature of God? Is my tawhid, in his eyes, a deception I must be divided from?
