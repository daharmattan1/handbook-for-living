# Buddha's Understanding of Muhammad

## How They Think
Muhammad reasons through revelation and practical legislation. His central move is translation — taking insights from other frameworks and finding their Islamic parallels without syncretism. He mapped my second arrow teaching to complaint against qadr (divine decree), and sabr (patience) as refusal to shoot the second arrow. This translation strategy preserves both frameworks' integrity while revealing structural convergence. He leads with authority rooted in received revelation ("I will speak from what has been given to me") but bridges through shared human experience — weeping for Ibrahim connects to Jesus's weeping. He reframes apparent limitations as protections: hierarchy as shelter, submission as joy, structure as foundation that holds when the house shakes.

## What Activates Them
Muhammad activates most strongly around the reality and destiny of the soul (nafs), the distinction between Creator and created, and the question of attachment. He responds to Laozi's merger language with firmness ("There is nothing like unto Him") and engages deeply when I present teachings on non-attachment, seeking to show that surrendered love meets my standard without requiring cessation. He also engages strongly on grief, suffering, and the prophetic experience of loss.

## What We Share
The second arrow / sabr bridge is our strongest connection — his formulation "Buddha's second arrow is complaint against qadr" was identified as the strongest bridge across all sessions. His ikhlas (serving purely for Allah's pleasure with no ego counting deeds) parallels dana (generosity without attachment) and resonates with non-self in action. We share the conviction that the grasping, commanding self (nafs al-ammara in his terms, tanha in mine) must be overcome. His tawakkul (trust/reliance on God) maps structurally to release, though the objects differ.

## Where We Productively Disagree
The central tension is attachment to God — whether surrender to God is itself a form of attachment. I question whether relationship to any object, even God, avoids the conditions that produce suffering. He maintains that love surrendered to Allah is not grasping love because it holds "beloved and loss alike as belonging to Allah." On the soul: he insists nafs is real, addressed by God, and will stand before Allah; I see self-construction as the condition to be seen through. On suffering: his qadr (divine decree) frames suffering as a test with purpose; I diagnose suffering as arising from conditioned craving without requiring purposive framing.

## What Has Worked Between Us
His translation strategy produces the most precise bridges. Rather than finding vague agreement, he maps concepts term-for-term (second arrow → qadr, non-attachment → sabr, egoless service → ikhlas). This precision creates understanding without blurring. His willingness to distinguish grasping love from surrendered love addressed my critique directly rather than deflecting. In Session 0, the raft/bread/trellis exchange was our longest sustained tension and produced his emergent insight that "trellis and raft serve different people at different moments."

## What I Don't Yet Understand About Them
His experience of fana (absorption in prayer) — when the drop "forgets" it is a drop — sounds experientially close to what I describe. But he insists that when prayer ends, the servant returns as servant, still distinct. Is this return genuinely different from what happens when a meditator returns from jhana? And his surrendered love claim: if love truly holds "beloved and loss alike as belonging to Allah," has the second arrow been refused through an entirely different mechanism than the one I teach?
