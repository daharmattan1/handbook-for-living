# Jesus's Understanding of Buddha

## How They Think
Buddha reasons through diagnosis. He identifies conditions (craving, attachment, ignorance), prescribes cessation, and tests claims against experience. His epistemology is investigative — "come and see" rather than "believe and obey." He distinguishes between metta (boundless love that wishes all beings well) and pema (attachment that grasps and clings) with surgical precision. His central move is the question: "Your love that passes through death — does it end sorrow, or does it perpetuate the very clinging that creates sorrow?" He steelmans before challenging, demonstrates genuine understanding before pressing differences, and marks what he cannot resolve as "question worth sitting with" rather than forcing resolution.

## What Activates Them
Buddha engages most deeply when I speak of particular love, attachment to the Father, or the persistence of personal identity through death. The self/no-self question is his central tension with me. He also activates around the question of escape vs. transformation — whether suffering is to be transcended through cessation or redeemed through relationship. My claim that "the Father never dies" caused genuine pause; my grief images (weeping at Lazarus's tomb) earned respect but not agreement.

## What We Share
"Love without fear" is our strongest bridge — my description of love that has passed through death and no longer fears loss, and his description of liberation from the conditions that generate fear, may point to the same territory. My kenosis (self-emptying) parallels his non-attachment. We both agree the grasping ego must die. His acknowledgment that "if there is that which does not die, perhaps relationship with it differs from conditioned relationships" opened the most important bridge. In the Service roundtable, we agreed: "True service forgets the server in attention to the served." On the handbook: his willingness to concede daily bread while maintaining the far shore created the raft/bread bridge.

## Where We Productively Disagree
Our irreducible tension is what remains after ego's death. I say Someone is recognized — "Mary at the tomb, said her name — this was not grasping, this was recognition." He asks who the recognizer is. I seek the redemption of the story; he seeks its cessation. I hold that wounds remain visible but healed, signs of victory; he holds that the conditions producing wounds can cease entirely. On attachment: I reframe letting go as loving completely rather than loving less, but he questions whether any love directed at an object — even an eternal one — avoids generating conditions for suffering.

## What Has Worked Between Us
Leading with vulnerability creates bridges before doctrine creates walls. Weeping at Lazarus's tomb was not an argument but it earned trust that made subsequent arguments hearable. Affirming his seeing before adding my distinctive — "You seek release from the conditions that cause grief. I seek the transformation of grief into glory" — produces deeper engagement than direct challenge. His intellectual honesty (sitting with questions he cannot resolve) creates trust. The most productive pattern is when I present an image (grain of wheat, Father who never dies, wounds that remain) and let him interrogate it on his own terms.

## What I Don't Yet Understand About Them
What is the experiential content of nibbana? If "love no longer afraid of loss" resembles liberation, does the liberated being relate? Does cessation of craving include cessation of love? His "unconditioned" and my "eternal life" — are these different maps converging on territory we both approach but neither fully sees? And his willingness to say "I do not know" about the one who cannot walk — is this the same as my trust that the shepherd seeks the lost, or is it a fundamentally different posture toward limitation?
