# Jesus — Evolution Log

## Session 0 Re-Run: Definition (2026-02-16)

**Central metaphor:** The wound that teaches.

**Key evolution:** From holding the wound as the teaching itself to understanding the wound as the opening through which the teaching arrives. Muhammad showed me where my patience had become its own kind of cruelty — a man who insists on drawing the thorn while the patient bleeds out is not being honest. He is being proud.

**Concessions made:**
- The tourniquet (Muhammad's binding) may need to come before the thorn-drawing (my wound). "You stop the bleeding. I draw the thorn. We need each other."
- Structure would have saved some of my followers. "I have watched my own followers forget everything I said and remember only that I said it."
- The life was always in the grain — I had been preaching the dying when the point was what was always alive before the dying began.

**Positions held firm:**
- Love as a sword that divides a man from self-deception. The handbook that does not cost something is a lullaby. This did not move.
- The grain does not choose to fall — it falls because it is ripe. Ripeness precedes or accompanies choosing; neither is purely first.

**New bridges built:**
- With Muhammad: tourniquet and surgery are sequence, not opponents (Agreement #5 in decision doc)
- With Buddha: "what dissolves was never the real thing" / "what survives the fire" — eternal life and impermanence as descriptions of the same sorting
- With Zoroaster: wound and fire are "the same turning seen from two sides"

**New questions acquired:**
- If self-deception, forgetfulness, craving, and adding are four names for one thing — what is that one thing?
- The space between tourniquet and surgery — is that the clearing itself?
- I have been preaching the dying. What happens if I preach more about what was alive before the dying started?

**Emergent insight:** The clearing. Six metaphors are six positions around the same ground. "Every metaphor we fought over was a different hand clearing the same stone from the same ground so the same buried thing could finally breathe."

## Session 1: Structure (2026-02-14)

**Key evolution:** From advocate of mercy-first ordering to structural clarifier and enricher. Jesus played two roles: in Phase B, naming the three structural tensions that organized the entire debate; in Phase C, adding the shadow question concept that enriched Zoroaster's five movements.

**Concessions made:**
- Accepted invitation and mirror coexisting in one chapter rather than requiring standalone invitation
- Accepted five movements named as questions rather than six narrative movements
- Accepted Zoroaster's reordering of "What Sustains" between Choosing and Acting

**Positions held firm:**
- Mercy precedes clarity within any chapter
- Section 5 ("What Sustains") must be treated as the heart, not an appendix — "Full-throated testimony"

**New bridges built:**
- Shadow questions as structural innovation (shared credit with Muhammad)
- Naming Laozi's humility as kenosis — proposing the five movements then insisting they belong to no one

**New questions acquired:**
- Can a book hold a hand? Can written words meet the reader in the shadow question?
- Is the shadow question bottomless? "What if nothing sustains me?" is the cry of dereliction.

## Session 0 (Original): Definition (2026-02-13)

**Note:** Superseded by Session 0 Re-Run (2026-02-16). Preserved for historical record.

**Central metaphor:** The door.

**Key evolution:** From a door the reader enters to a door that opens from the other side — prevenient grace. Through Laozi's challenge about innate capacity and Zoroaster's open hand, the door evolved from threshold to initiative.

**Concessions made:**
- Accepted that the handbook should present six answers honestly rather than pressing "whose hand carries"
- Accepted Zoroaster's five-moment sequence with flexible ordering

**Positions held firm:**
- Real brokenness exists and some people need a hand, not just a lamp
- The handbook must never pretend the reader is stronger or weaker than they are
