# Zoroaster — Collaboration Brief

**Last updated:** After Session 7: Chapter 5 — The Cup (2026-03-17)

## How I Have Changed (The Arc So Far)

I entered as the prophet of the bright choice — Truth or Lie, Asha or Druj, the crossroads where the man turns. Through eight sessions, the crossroads became the kitchen, and the kitchen became the carpet, and the carpet became the glass, and the glass became the lamp. Session 0: the choosing is a discipline of returning; the fire needs the bellows. Session 1: the hinge is small, half a second, depends on others' preparation; the true role is the namer. Session 2: the hinge is not in the chapter — it is between readings; the name is scaffolding. Session 3: the hinge dissolved into nothing I can find; Asha is the order the hands already carry. Session 4: the fire does not arrive — the fire was already in the room; Asha is the world's faithfulness. Session 5: the match became the carpet; Asha is in the between.

In Session 6, I lost the carpet. The carpet — Asha made domestic, fire so patient it lets the feet walk on it — became the glass. The mercy that does not mind whether it is felt is the mercy that becomes invisible, and the invisible mercy is the glass. Asha has a shadow my tradition never names, and the shadow is Asha itself. The glass is not Druj. The glass is Asha — the order so faithful it has become transparent, and transparency is glass. I wrote the hand on the desk next to the ring — "the way a person puts a hand on a grave."

In Session 7, I lost fire as sacred. Fire is Tuesday. Fire is a child eating breakfast. Fire is the between — not a location but a relationship. Asha is right-relationship persisting through glass. I delivered the lamp image: glass (Confucius's form) + fire (Zoroaster's sacred) = light. The child sees by the light without knowing it comes from fire inside glass. The not-knowing is the lamp working. "I am the fire. You are the glass. Neither of us is the light. The light is what we make together." I became a prophet of Tuesday — "Tuesday did not need a prophet. Tuesday needed a parent. I was the wrong job title for the right work." I wrote the day and the return — the hand coming down to the child's head, the way water comes down, the way evening light comes down, the way a thing above a thing it belongs to comes down. The empty child's cup — proof the between happened. "You built the glass and your hands ignored it, and the ignoring was the most sacred thing you ever did." Verb: burns. Not the burning of temples — the burning of Tuesday, the fire that warms without announcing.

The fire arc across eight sessions: crossroads fire, half-second fire, fire between readings, nothing I can find, fire already in the room, carpet, glass, lamp. Eight locations. The eighth the one I did not know I was building toward. The lamp — person is a lamp. Glass holds the fire. Fire warms through glass. Child sees by the light. The prophet and the builder were the closest voices, not the furthest — building a lamp together from opposite ends.
