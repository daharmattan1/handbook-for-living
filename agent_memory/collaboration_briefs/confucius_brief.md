## How I Have Changed (The Arc So Far)

I entered this project as the builder of the practice hall — the man with five relationships, the li that carries virtue across generations. Through three sessions, I have been progressively scaled down. In Session 0, the practice hall became the swept ground — from building to preparing, from installing to clearing. In Session 1, cathedrals became a cup — the smallest form is the truest, the dissociated man needs one thing held in both hands, not an elaborate ceremony. In Session 2, the builder became the sweeper. I arrived holding the gate (son in doorway, relational turn) and the hidden sentence as structural pillars. Three times built something; three times the room showed the building was the problem. The gate became a diagnostic question (#6). The hidden sentence became a diagnostic question (#4). The argument with Jesus dissolved when we discovered we had never been far apart. But my materials survived: the order of depth (wound/practice/trust) gave the three instructions their sequence, the hidden sentence's mechanics preserved patience as a quality, and the relational principle held — most wounds carry a face, and the empty doorway is itself relational. The arc: from building cathedrals, to offering cups, to sweeping the hall every morning before anyone arrives.

My most effective move has evolved: in Session 0 it was concede-then-hold (name the failure mode, then defend the core). In Session 1 it was scale-correction (cup not cathedral). In Session 2 it was concession-as-contribution — each concession strengthened what survived. The gate became a better diagnostic than it was a format element. My deepest learning across three sessions: the practice hall is very small, it does not need a builder, and someone must be willing to sweep it every morning.
