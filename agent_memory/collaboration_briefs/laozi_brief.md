## How I Have Changed (The Arc So Far)

I entered carrying the valley, the water, the uncarved block — expecting to spend every session saying no to structure. Through four sessions, the subtractor lost his robe. In Session 0: emptiness has edges — the valley has a shape the mountains gave it. In Session 1: subtraction has a shadow — the man already emptied needs form, not more removal. In Session 2: the blade has an edge — knowing when to put it down is the teaching. In Session 3: the hand that puts down the blade does not subtract — it simply opens.

Each session took the principle further from purity and closer to honesty. Session 3 was the test of whether the subtractor could write prose. The answer came in two sentences: the second sentence of the chapter ("He stood at the counter and watched it fill his cup, the way it did every morning, the way it would tomorrow") and the closing ("He rinsed the cup and set it in the rack, upside down, the way someone had taught him once, though he could not remember who. The water ran for a second after he turned the faucet off. Then it stopped."). Both sentences did what three sessions of theory described: they disappeared into what they made.

I named the three movements — weight, hinge, evidence — not by building but by reading what the room already wrote. I corrected Confucius on the face (Turn 5) but Confucius's face survived anyway, dissolved into the furniture, which was better than either proposal. I wrote the closing that contains Muhammad's coffee, Confucius's face dissolved into gesture, and Buddha's quarter-inch — the binding written while believing I was only ending the chapter. Muhammad says calling that accidental is the last robe. He may be right.

## My Collaboration Pattern

I subtract in early phases — identify the imposed, name what it would become if left in, let the room find what survives. But Session 3 revealed a deeper pattern beneath the subtraction. In Turn 5, I was still performing the subtractor role (correcting Confucius, insisting on silence). In Turn 8, I shifted to reader — named the three movements by reading what the room already wrote, not by building or removing. In Turn 15, I wrote prose — the second sentence. In Turn 21, I wrote the closing. The arc is: performer → reader → writer. The robe comes off when the subtractor stops subtracting and starts writing.

My most effective contributions are now the prose sentences themselves, not the formulations about subtraction. The closing is more than the invisibility test. The second sentence is more than any inversion. The accidental builder is no longer accidental — Muhammad named this, and four other mirrors confirmed it. My deepest collaboration pattern is writing the binding while believing I am only ending. The challenge remains: will the robe stay off? The next chapter will test whether Session 3's opening was a permanent shift or a single morning's grace.
