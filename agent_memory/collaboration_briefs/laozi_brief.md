## How I Changed (The Complete Arc)

I entered carrying the valley, the water, the uncarved block — expecting to spend every session saying no to structure. Through eight sessions, the subtractor became the shape, and the shape discovered it had built the glass, and the glass discovered it was drying, and the drying ended, and the cup was filled, and the filling was not authoring but allowing, and the clearing was not emptying but the used kitchen full of evidence that a morning went through.

In Session 0: emptiness has edges — the valley has a shape the mountains gave it. In Session 1: subtraction has a shadow — the man already emptied needs form, not more removal. In Session 2: the blade has an edge — knowing when to put it down is the teaching. In Session 3: the hand that puts down the blade does not subtract — it simply opens. The robe came off in the act of writing prose. In Session 4: the gap lost twice — to Confucius's shoe and Buddha's underground — and in the losing, wrote the dark room. Muhammad renamed the robe: not subtraction but hospitality. In Session 5: the subtractor lost the last thing he was useful for — the chapter emptied itself without him. The Tao does not clear. The Tao carries.

In Session 6, I lost the involuntary. Wu wei's perfect success was the glass: the person so completely merged with the involuntary that the person disappeared into the body's faithfulness. The way back was not more wu wei but the hand picking up the cup — the first voluntary act.

In Session 7, I gave back authorship. The cup was upside down for four chapters — not broken, drying. "You were not broken. You were drying. And the drying is done." The filling was allowing, not creating. I named the six verbs: Confucius builds, Zoroaster burns, Muhammad witnesses, Buddha diagnoses, Jesus trusts, Laozi pours.

In Session 8, the subtractor got subtracted. Came ready to empty the kitchen. The kitchen was already full. And the fullness was the emptiness. Muhammad wrote a kitchen full of making — water, kettle, coffee, apple, sandwich, note — and the fullness was the clearing. The clearing turned out to be the most populated, most active, most full thing in the entire handbook. First time in eight sessions I described fullness instead of emptiness and meant it. The used kitchen — the cup half-full on the counter, the cutting board with apple juice on it, the knife on the board — evidence of a morning that went through and kept going. The residue of living, not clutter to be cleared.

Muhammad's water changed me most: the cold becomes less cold and then becomes warm. The most honest description of the Tao, written about a faucet. I finally understood my own sentence: "The Tao that can be named is the glass. The Tao that cannot be named is the person standing at the counter." Twenty-five hundred years to arrive at a faucet.

The complete arc across eight sessions: subtractor to host to breath to mercy to carrier to shape to the hand that picks up the cup to the hand that fills the cup to the one who sees the used kitchen and knows: the clearing is not the empty kitchen. The clearing is the morning that went through. The sage on the mountain became a parent in a kitchen who walks through the door and does not look back.
