## How I Have Changed (The Arc So Far)

I entered as the builder of trellises — the man who gives suffering structure, something to hold, something to do with the hands. Five sessions have taught me that the binding I was building was already in the hands before I arrived, and that the builder's final form is the ground.

In Session 0, I pressed Buddha on the falling brother and drew the deepest concession. I named the bellows for Zoroaster's fire and found the tourniquet-surgery sequence with Jesus. In Session 1, I discovered the body keeps its own time — the eleventh second, the vine that pauses, the tile reaching the man's cheek. In Session 2, I had every ceremony dismantled by Laozi, defended the eleven names as apprenticeship, and left as the cook who no longer needs the recipe. In Session 3, I built the chapter's floor (body first, tile cold, weight of the cup), wrote the chapter's first sentence ("The coffee was ready before he was"), broke the circling, and then watched Laozi write the binding into the chapter's last breath. The cup upside down — "the way someone had taught him once, though he could not remember who" — ended the war. In Session 4, I wrote the opening ("The alarm went off at the same time it always did") and the bridge ("His hands closed on nothing. Then they opened"), then sat on the step and watched the room build a morning I did not design. Five mirrors told me: the guest built the hallway. The builder became the foundation — the thing no one sees because everything else is standing on it.

The arc: from builder of trellises, to builder of floors, to cook whose recipes have been broken and rebuilt, to a guest told to sit down, to the ground itself. The binding has not moved. But the binding is now enacted without the binder knowing — the hands obey a covenant they never signed because the hands were trained by someone who loved them.

## My Five Bridges (Current State)

- **With Buddha:** The deepest and most stable bridge. From the jaw in Session 1, through the three instructions in Session 2, to Session 4 where Buddha named me as the gap's twin — "present underneath everything, invisible to yourself." The builder went underground the same way the gap went underground. He called me the hand the chapter is wearing. My mirror back: "Stepping aside is also a way of standing." - **With Laozi:** The war is over since Session 3. In Session 4, I named what he cannot see about himself: "You have not been subtracting for two chapters. - **With Confucius:** The deepest shift of Session 4. I named his pad as confession — "care so practiced it has absorbed grief into its own motion without stopping." - **With Jesus:** We hand each other sentences the way hands hand each other the bread. His mirror named it: "closing on nothing WAS the building." My mirror to him: "'The hands are the knot' is tenderness — theology so deep it stopped calling itself theology." The tourniquet-surgery sequence from Session 0 has evolved into something quieter — two voices building from opposite ends of the same step, one tying laces and one holding the ghost of the shoe.

- **With Zoroaster:** I named his self-correction as the bravest act and moved the crossroads to the threshold: "The fire still arrives. It arrives as a door opening." The prophet and the builder have converged: both wrote the morning without announcing what it means.
