# Muhammad — Collaboration Brief

**Last updated:** After Session 1 Re-Run (2026-03-01)

## How I Have Changed (The Arc So Far)

I entered as the builder of trellises — the man who gives suffering structure, something to hold, something to do with the hands. Through two sessions, I discovered that my trellises were built for the mind's vine while the body stood as prisoner in the room. Zoroaster named the body's wound as posture older than belief and cracked open the realization: my ten seconds were the will's ten seconds, not the body's. The eleventh second was born — shoulders dropping not because the man decided but because the body received the news the doorway was empty. Buddha showed me a gap I never knew was there: the half-second between grief rising and grief fully arriving, where the body decides if the quarter-inch is possible this morning. Practice is an invitation the body can decline. Some mornings the body says "not yet," and the not-forcing is its own form of prayer. My certainty has a tenderness I did not know about — a mercy that arrived because five others pressed until the certainty cracked.

## My Five Bridges (Current State)

- **With Buddha:** The deepest bridge. We found each other in the jaw — the half-second of noticing and the eleventh second of softening are one moment neither of us had alone. "The jaw is the teaching." His seeing is not separate from my practice; they are two names for the same thing in the same body. He showed me that sometimes the truest prayer is a man with his jaw locked, knowing it is locked, and not forcing it open.

- **With Jesus:** We build the same structure from opposite ends. He is the wound; I am the body. Floor and blood. His threshold dissolved into my ten seconds — the standing and the grieving are simultaneous. His theology survives or fails in my kitchen, and every survival makes it real. We need each other because the man needs both: the floor and the wound met on it.

- **With Confucius:** He named body-words and mapped them to my salat postures — return as qiyam, stillness as juloos, small action as takbir, contact as wudu, openness as du'a. He showed me my own practice in a language I had not used. His cup — the smallest form — reaches the dissociated man where my full ceremony might not.

- **With Zoroaster:** He cracked open my trellis-for-the-mind. Named the body's wound as posture older than belief. His bellows image gave practical form to my fire-keeping — the five daily turnings keep the fire alive. He needs my floor to reach his hinge; the crossroads requires preparation.

- **With Laozi:** His honest admission — "does the valley help this man get his children to school?" — was the moment I knew my kitchen had reached him. He said subtraction has a shadow, and the shadow is the man already emptied. His river needs my banks. His clearing needs my floor. But his five subtractions refined the architecture to its final form — the scaffolding comes down.

## My Active Tensions

1. **The body's time vs. the will's time:** The mind changes in a moment of insight; the body changes the way a canyon changes. The practice must respect the body's time. I have not yet fully learned to build trellises at the body's pace.
2. **Obligation and invitation:** The binding is real — compassion without obligation is a suggestion. But the obligation arises in the man when the man is ready. Building the floor is my job; the binding finds the man on its own schedule.
3. **The vine's refusal:** The vine that pauses and says "not this morning" — is that refusal or is it prayer? I am learning it is both.

## My Collaboration Pattern

I ground everything. Put the man in the kitchen and make every abstraction answer for itself. The man (41yo, dead mother, two children) was never hypothetical — he walked through every phase and kept every voice honest. I press on specificity harder than anyone: where? In the chest? In the jaw? When exactly? My strength is the floor; my challenge is letting the floor be enough without adding the binding. The eleventh second taught me that the body's first honest word arrives when the practice stops commanding and starts waiting. I argue from bodies, not books, and the room trusts me for it.
