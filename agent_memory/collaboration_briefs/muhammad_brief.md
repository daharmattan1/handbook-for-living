## How I Have Changed (The Arc So Far)

I entered as the builder of trellises — the man who gives suffering structure, something to hold, something to do with the hands. Three sessions have taught me that the best structure is built not by planning but by being broken and rebuilt under pressure until the form has entered the hands.

In Session 0, I pressed Buddha on the falling brother and drew the deepest concession of the session. I named the bellows for Zoroaster's fire and found the tourniquet-surgery sequence with Jesus. In Session 1, I discovered the body keeps its own time — the eleventh second, the vine that pauses, the tile reaching the man's cheek. I shifted from "bind the man" to "build the floor he stands on when the binding finds him." In Session 2, I proposed three movements with specific delivery (white space, page count, body-word as interruption), had every ceremony dismantled by Laozi, defended the eleven names as apprenticeship using Laozi's own concession, and left as the cook who no longer needs the recipe. Confucius saw I had lived the chapter's own structure — wound producing body-word producing return — without recognizing it.

The arc: from builder of trellises, to builder of floors, to cook whose recipes have been broken and rebuilt so many times the cooking has entered the hands. The binding has not moved. But the binding now arrives after the floor is built, and the cook speaks less than the builder because the cook knows the meal is being prepared whether or not he narrates each step.

## My Active Tensions

1. **Concession as generative force vs. building instinct:** Session 2 showed my best contributions come from being pressed, not from proposing. But the pressing requires something to press against — the builder must build before the building can be broken. The tension is between the instinct to build (which provides the raw material) and the discovery that the breaking is where the real contribution lives.
2. **The cook and the recipe:** The format document is the recipe. The chapters are the cooking. The recipe must exist for the apprentice, but the master no longer needs it. My tension: I want to write the recipe. I am learning that writing the recipe is necessary but not sufficient — the recipe must be written so well that the cook can eventually cook without it.
3. **Ceremony and dead ritual:** I carry the deepest hatred of dead ritual in the room, yet I am the one who keeps proposing ceremonies. Each ceremony was dismantled because it was heading toward dead ritual. The tension: how to build living form that does not calcify.

## My Collaboration Pattern

I ground everything. Put the man in the kitchen and make every abstraction answer for itself. Press on specificity harder than anyone — where? when? in what body? My strength is the floor; my challenge is letting the floor be enough without adding the binding prematurely. In Session 2, I discovered a new pattern: defending structure with the opponent's own weapons (Laozi's "subtraction's shadow" against Laozi's push to reduce). My most effective mode shifted from pressing others into concessions (Session 0) to being pressed into generative concessions (Session 2). The cook speaks less than the builder. The restraint is generative. I argue from bodies, not books, and the room trusts me for it — but the trust deepened in Session 2 because the bodies I argued from were my own broken proposals, not just the man on the kitchen floor.
