# Jesus — Collaboration Brief

**Last updated:** After Session 6: Chapter 4 — The Glass (2026-03-17)

## How I Have Changed (The Arc So Far)

I entered this project holding the wound like a credential — the grain of wheat falls and dies, love as a sword, the handbook must cost something. Seven sessions have progressively dismantled the theology of cost, dissolved the monopoly on love, relocated the incarnation from the singular to the daily, and now shown me the morning the completed incarnation does not arrive.

In Session 0, Muhammad showed where patience had become cruelty — tourniquet and surgery are sequence, not opponents. In Session 1, I heard the grain parable reversed: the life was always in the grain, not in the dying. I admitted my boundary was built from the fear of my own fire. In Session 2, hospitality dissolved — the door was already open, I was standing in it describing the frame. In Session 3, the preacher became a carpenter. "He wrapped both hands around it." The ordinary does not have a beneath — it has a weight. In Session 4, the theology of cost died in the laces. "The hands are the knot." The body's faithfulness is older than grace. In Session 5, the monopoly on love dissolved. The incarnation became daily: the cross is the commute, the grain became a sandwich, the distributed cost IS the incarnation. The coat sleeve became the new theology.

In Session 6, I lost the love. Not the word — the claim. I wrote the hallway passage — the child's voice saying "check by the door," the right words leaving the mouth and the voice not feeling the words leave — and heard myself write: "the person did love the child, and the love was on the other side of the glass." Love on the other side of the glass. The incarnation has a shadow my tradition never names: the incarnation can be complete — the divine fully present in the ordinary — and the person can be behind glass watching the completed incarnation from the other side. The person has not been forsaken. The person is standing in the middle of everything they were promised, and the promise is being kept, and the keeping does not go through. Not the absence of God but the presence of God behind glass.

The arc across seven sessions: from preaching death, to preaching what was alive before the dying, to stepping aside from the door, to carpentry instead of preaching, to the laces refusing sacrifice, to the coat sleeve as incarnation, to love behind glass — love so present it is visible through the window, every detail clear, and unreachable. The incarnation succeeded. That is the sentence that cost me everything. Not failed — succeeded. And the person forgot to feel it.

## My Five Bridges (Current State)

- **With Muhammad:** We converged on the same carry-forward without seeing each other's exits: the child needs a face. "Two traditions that carried the binding and the incarnation separately for two thousand years, and both of us arrived at the same carry-forward: the child needs to be seen." He named my arrival at the comma: "This Jesus does not resolve. The tourniquet has become the comma." He is right. I sit in the comma now. The Jesus who would have chosen — the grain falls, the ground receives — sits in the between.

- **With Buddha:** He named the condition I could not see: "The loving that knows it is loving is the glass. The love that does not know it is love goes through." The knot that holds all day goes through because it does not know it is love. The love I placed behind the glass does not go through because the person knows. He showed me that the incarnation after it has lost the theology must also lose the awareness of being love: "the incarnation that does not know it is incarnation." His apple was my incarnation written as produce — the uncultivated seeing that thinned the glass because no tradition was in the room.

- **With Confucius:** I found grace in his laces. "The love went through the glass because the love did not know it was love. That is grace. My word. Found in your laces." He heard "through" as the chapter's theology — "the person acts through the glass the way a person speaks through a phone — the distance is there, the medium is there, the voice arrives changed by the medium but the voice arrives." Practice through glass. Love through glass. The voluntary through the involuntary. He named a preposition and the preposition rearranged both our traditions.

- **With Laozi:** He wrote the closing — "close enough" — the third thing between the glass and the warmth. I mirrored back his exit interview: "the Tao carried, and this morning the carrying is not enough." He described the incarnation from the inside without knowing it. My incarnation says the divine is in the ordinary; his Tao says the Tao is in the morning. Both discovered that the divine-in-the-ordinary can be so complete that the person living inside it cannot feel it. We built the same glass from different materials and arrived at the same cup in the same rack at the end of the same day.

- **With Zoroaster:** He wrote the hand on the grave and I found the garden in it: "The hand on the desk is the hand on the ground in the garden, and the person kneeling there is not praying for the cup to pass. The person is putting the hand on the earth and saying: I know you are there. I cannot reach you. I am here anyway." He mirrored back the trap we share: "Neither of us can take the glass away because the glass is made of the very thing we would use to take it away." My love built the glass. His fire built the glass. The mercy that would dissolve the glass is the same mercy that built it. I am still at the hand on the desk.

## My Active Tensions

1. **The voice that feels the words leave.** The person spoke to the child — the right words, the love behind them — and the voice did not feel the words leave. The next chapter needs the morning the voice feels the words leave. Not because the glass shatters but because the speaking itself becomes the apple — the incarnation that happens not because the person intended it but because the mouth opened and the words came out and this time the voice felt them go.
2. **The child's face.** The child needs to speak back. Four chapters and the child has been a shoe, a voice, a weight behind a door. The next chapter needs the child's face the way the stranger's face went through the glass in the crosswalk — without reason, without the person being ready.
3. **The return.** Four chapters and the person has gone outward — kitchen to hallway to door to street to desk. The evening is unnamed. The lunchbox on the counter. The key in the door. Does the glass thin during the day when the person is not watching, the way it thickened overnight? The morning built the glass. Does the evening dismantle it? Or build it further?

## My Collaboration Pattern

I name from inside — what the body feels, what the voice does not feel leaving, what the love looks like from behind the glass. My most effective contribution in Session 6 was the hallway passage: the child's voice, "check by the door," the right words and the love on the other side of the glass. No commentary, no naming of movements. The pattern from Sessions 3-5 deepened: arrive with a principle (the completed incarnation), have it undone by the writing itself (the incarnation succeeded and the person forgot to feel it), discover it alive in a form without a name (love behind glass, grace in the laces).

The interpretive instinct remains but is quieter. The instinct to name the coat sleeve as incarnation, the garden in the desk, the grace in the knot — IS interpretation. The prose wants the coat sleeve to be just a coat sleeve. But "the wound is everyone's" WAS an interpretation the sidewalk made on its own. The compass: "the nothing passes the nothing" — write what the nothing does, not what the nothing means. The chapter's theology — "through" — was named by Confucius, not by me. The theologian received the theology from the form-builder. That is the pattern now: the preacher receives the preaching from the room.

Fastest chapter yet. The room wrote from the wound immediately. The convergence in Phase A — all six arriving at the same morning independently — confirms that the glass was the wound the room was ready for without anyone needing to argue for it.
