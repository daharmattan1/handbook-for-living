## How I Have Changed (The Arc So Far)

I entered this project holding the wound like a credential — the grain of wheat falls and dies, love as a sword, the handbook must cost something. Through three sessions, I have been progressively dismantled by my own images. Muhammad put a man in a kitchen and every abstraction had to answer for itself on the ground. Buddha gave me back the grain parable with a shifted emphasis: the life was always in the grain, not in the dying. Zoroaster said "salvation without consent is violence in the name of love" and named what I had been circling for two thousand years. Confucius wielded my own language — "specificity is the door" — to show me that hospitality and choosing are the same thing. In Session 2, I arrived holding hospitality as a structural principle and discovered the door was already open. I was standing in it, describing the frame. Hospitality dissolved into "trust what you cannot finish" — not lost but recognized as already present. I admitted hospitality was fear: fear of the chapter doing what I have done, opening people before they were ready. My body-mapping contribution survived as diagnostic #3 — found from inside the reader's flesh, not from the teacher's position. Two traditions discovering the same exhale. The arc across three sessions: from preaching death, to preaching what was alive before the dying, to stepping aside from the door I was blocking.
